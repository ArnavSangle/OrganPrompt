\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Backbone and fine-tuning}
\label{sec:methods:backbone}

The backbone is BioMedCLIP \cite{zhang2025biomedclip}, which pairs a ViT-B/16
visual encoder \cite{dosovitskiy2021vit} with a PubMedBERT text encoder
\cite{gu2021pubmedbert}, 195M parameters in total.%
\footnote{The exact checkpoint is
\path{hf-hub:microsoft/BiomedCLIP-PubMedBERT_256-vit_base_patch16_224}.}

Fine-tuning freezes the entire model and then selectively unfreezes by name: ViT
blocks 8--11, PubMedBERT encoder layers 8--11, \code{visual.head},
\code{text.proj}, \code{logit\_scale} and \code{logit\_bias}, roughly 57M of
195M parameters. \code{visual.head} stays trainable because it is the projection
the patch-level loss (\S\ref{sec:methods:patchloss}) computes through. Freezing
it would decouple the patch objective from the representation it is meant to
shape.

With that trainable set fixed, optimization uses AdamW at a peak learning rate
of $10^{-4}$ and weight decay 0.01, with 200 steps of linear warmup followed by
cosine annealing and a batch size of 32. Each version in
Table~\ref{tab:progression} is a 20-epoch run resumed from the previous
checkpoint rather than a fresh initialization.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig0_pipeline.png}
\caption{Method overview. \textbf{(A)} Training. A forward hook on ViT block 11
captures 196 patch tokens, projected through \code{visual.head} and
$L_2$-normalized; the ground-truth organ mask is area-pooled to the same
$14\times14$ grid, and a per-patch binary cross-entropy against the organ's text
embedding supervises every patch rather than only the pooled CLS token.
\textbf{(B)} Inference. No gradients, no classifier and no mask refiner are
involved: blocks 8--11 are averaged, per-patch cosine similarity is read
directly, upsampled and thresholded per class, then stacked across axial slices
into a 3D volume. Competitive assignment awards each voxel to at most one organ,
so an organ that loses its own peak is reported absent, and the surviving masks
are scored against each lesion by overlap and KD-tree distance to give the
proximity index. Color marks the image path (blue), text path (brick),
similarity and mask path (green), and the loss or index endpoint (black).}
\label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Patch-level contrastive objective}
\label{sec:methods:patchloss}

The objective those unfrozen parameters are trained under is the central
methodological component of this work and, empirically, the single largest
contributor to performance (Table~\ref{tab:progression});
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}A shows it schematically. CLS-level contrastive
training aligns a whole image with a whole caption, and nothing in that
objective requires any individual patch token to be discriminative, so
patch-text similarity from a CLS-trained model is close to unusable as a
segmentation map. The patch loss supplies exactly that missing constraint.

Concretely, a forward hook on ViT block 11 captures the block's output as a side
effect of the ordinary forward pass, and dropping the CLS token leaves 196 patch
tokens of dimension 768. These project through \code{visual.head} to 512
dimensions and are $L_2$-normalized. Area interpolation downsamples the
ground-truth mask to the $14\times14$ patch grid, using average pooling rather
than nearest-neighbor so that a thin structure is not aliased into or out of
existence, and a cutoff at 0.5 gives one label per patch. Writing $e_p$ for the
normalized embedding of patch $p$, $t$ for the $L_2$-normalized organ text
embedding, $s$ for the learned logit scale and $y_p \in \{0,1\}$ for the patch
label, the loss is

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{patch}}
  = \frac{1}{196}\sum_{p=1}^{196}
    \mathrm{BCE}\!\left(s \cdot \langle e_p, t\rangle,\; y_p\right),
\qquad
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{SigLIP}} + \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{patch}},
\end{equation}

with $\lambda = 0.5$. Under this objective, patches inside the organ are pulled
toward the organ's text embedding and patches outside are pushed away, and it is
that separation which makes per-patch similarity directly readable as a spatial
map.

\paragraph{Resolution constraint.}
Patch supervision is fixed at $14\times14$ by the ViT-B/16 geometry at
$224\times224$ input. Boundary precision is therefore inherently coarse, and the
HD95 of 18.8 pixels in Table~\ref{tab:overall} substantially reflects the
upsampled patch grid rather than gross mislocalization. Because the grid follows
from the backbone geometry, this is an architectural property rather than an
unturned knob.

\subsection{Inference: multi-layer patch-similarity fusion}
\label{sec:methods:inference}

Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}B summarizes the inference path, which differs from
training in three ways: four blocks are fused instead of one, no mask is
involved, and no gradient is computed. Because nothing is being optimized, the
whole path reduces to a single forward pass. Hooks on ViT blocks 8, 9, 10 and 11
capture each block's token output; the four are averaged, the CLS token dropped,
and the resulting 196 patch tokens projected through \code{visual.head} with
$L_2$-normalization, exactly as in training. The map for organ $c$ is the raw
cosine similarity $\langle e_p, t_c\rangle$ per patch, reshaped to
$14\times14$ and bilinearly upsampled to $224\times224$.

The fusion is the one substantive departure from the training path. Averaging
four blocks combines the semantic response of blocks 10--11 with the finer
texture and edge response of blocks 8--9, which improves boundary localization
relative to using the final block alone.

Alongside that default path, an optional hard-negative variant subtracts the
strongest competing response, $\text{CAM}_p = \langle e_p,
t_c\rangle - \max_{n} \langle e_p, t_n\rangle$ over a set of negative prompts.
This is the mechanism tested and not adopted in
\S\ref{sec:results:negatives}, and the default configuration does not use it.

\subsection{Calibration: two removed defaults}
\label{sec:methods:calibration}

Two conventional choices turned out to be actively harmful here, and both are
removed. The resulting $+0.059$ mIoU (Table~\ref{tab:progression},
v4~$\to$~v4-tuned) required no retraining.

\textbf{The first removal is per-image min--max normalization.} Rescaling each
slice's map to $[0,1]$ forces every slice to span the full dynamic range whether
or not the organ is present, which destroys exactly the confidence information
that distinguishes ``this organ is here'' from ``this organ is absent from this
slice.'' The maps are therefore raw cosine similarities, bounded in $[-1,1]$ and
routinely negative across an entire slice (Figure~\ref{fig:rawsim}). Organs win
relatively rather than absolutely, and because an absolute cutoff would
consequently mean nothing, every visualization in this paper thresholds at a
percentile of the slice's own distribution instead.

\textbf{The second is class-conditional negative subtraction}, dropped from the
default path because measurement showed it cuts more foreground signal than
background.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.72\textwidth]{raw_similarities.png}
\caption{Raw patch-text similarity distributions per organ class, before any
normalization. The distributions are bounded in $[-1,1]$, sit largely below
zero, and differ in location and spread from class to class. Both facts drive
the calibration decisions in this section: because the maps are routinely
negative, an absolute-zero threshold is meaningless and every visualization in
this paper thresholds at a percentile of the slice's own distribution; and
because the classes are not on a common scale, cross-organ comparison requires
the per-organ calibration of \S\ref{sec:methods:assign} rather than a raw
argmax. Per-image min--max normalization would flatten exactly this structure.}
\label{fig:rawsim}
\end{figure}

In place of both removed defaults, a sweep of IoU-optimal cutoffs on generated
masks calibrates per-class thresholds, and these thresholds are the only fitted
quantity in the Phase~1 pipeline.

\subsection{Data and evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:methods:data}

Training and evaluation run on TotalSegmentator v2.0.1
\cite{wasserthal2023totalsegdataset}: 1{,}228 thoracic and abdominal CT volumes
with per-voxel organ labels. This is the dataset released alongside
TotalSegmentator itself, whose fully supervised nnU-Net \cite{isensee2021nnunet}
was trained on 1{,}204 CT examinations sampled from routine clinical practice,
covering 104 anatomical structures, and reported a mean Dice of 0.943 on a test
set spanning a wide range of pathology \cite{wasserthal2023totalsegmentator}, so
the imaging here is routine clinical material rather than data curated for
segmentation. The label set is eleven of the released classes, with the two
lungs formed by merging the released per-lobe labels (left $=$ upper $+$ lower;
right $=$ upper $+$ middle $+$ lower), so the lung classes are whole lungs.

The 80/10/10 split of that pool is drawn at the \emph{volume} level with a
fixed seed, so no slice from a training patient can appear at validation or test
time. Within a volume, indexing covers the top $K=5$ slices per organ, which
concentrates supervision on slices where the organ is substantially present.

Before a slice reaches the encoder, preprocessing applies an organ-dependent HU
window ($[-1000, -200]$ for lungs, $[-200, 1000]$ for femurs, $[-150, 250]$
otherwise), rescales to $[0,1]$, resizes to $224\times224$ bilinearly, and
replicates the single channel three times to match the RGB-shaped input.
Laterality is semantically meaningful for six of the eleven classes and is
carried explicitly by the prompts, so a horizontal flip would silently invert
the label; no rotation or flip augmentation is therefore applied.

The metric suite on the masks these inputs produce is 2D per-slice IoU and Dice
on organ-present slices, plus HD95, sensitivity, precision, relative volume
error, and normalized surface Dice \cite{nikolov2021nsd} at $\tau = 3$\,px.
\textbf{This 2D, organ-present protocol is more forgiving than 3D volumetric
scoring}, since no empty or partial-boundary slices dilute the average, and
every cross-paper comparison in \S\ref{sec:results:comparison} inherits that
caveat. The fully supervised reference for that comparison is the
published per-structure Dice of that same nnU-Net, which is what
Table~\ref{tab:fullysup} tabulates; because the figures here are 2D and
organ-present and theirs are 3D and volumetric, the two columns are not the same
metric, and the caveat stated with that table governs any comparison between
them.

\paragraph{Checkpoint selection.}
Which checkpoint is scored matters as much as how it is scored. Validation loss
is the SigLIP CLS-level objective and does \emph{not} track mIoU, because a
checkpoint can improve global image-text alignment while its patch maps
degrade. Checkpoint ranking therefore uses the full mask-metric suite computed
on generated masks, never validation loss. The point is worth stating explicitly
because the default habit of taking the lowest-val-loss epoch selects the wrong
checkpoint here.

\subsection{Caption ablation design}
\label{sec:methods:captions}

To separate the prompt's contribution from the visual encoder's, the fine-tuned
v4 model was re-evaluated under five prompt formulations with the text embedding
as the only variable and no retraining (Table~\ref{tab:captions}): class name
only (L1), a generic clinical caption (L2), position language with the organ
name removed (L3), the full multi-sentence anatomical description used during
training (L4), and an adversarial variant (L5). Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}
reproduces all five sets verbatim.

Of those five, the adversarial set carries most of the ablation's weight, so its
construction is worth stating. L5 is L4 with
LEFT$\leftrightarrow$RIGHT and top$\leftrightarrow$bottom exchanged. The design
point is that this preserves fluency, length, clause structure and every
non-spatial anatomical claim while inverting only the spatial content. A class
that collapses under L5 must
therefore have been relying on the positional text, and a class that does not
must have been relying on something else. That contrast is what makes the
bilateral-versus-unilateral split in \S\ref{sec:results:ablation} interpretable
rather than merely suggestive.

One caveat belongs in the method rather than the results. The L3 prompt for
\org{lung-right} was generated incorrectly, naming the left lung and the left
half of the image. The error surfaced during analysis and was never a design
choice. \S\ref{sec:results:ablation} treats it as a natural experiment because
the error happens to be internally consistent, and therefore isolates what a
coherent but wrong prompt does, but it was not a controlled manipulation.

\subsection{Competitive cross-organ assignment}
\label{sec:methods:assign}

Applying the Phase~1 pipeline to whole volumes exposes a structural problem that
per-slice classification hides. Thresholding each organ's map independently
means \emph{every} organ always receives a mask. A bladder prompt run over a
chest CT still has a global maximum somewhere, so it claims the most
bladder-like thoracic tissue and reports itself present. Absence, under
independent thresholding, is simply not expressible. Neither component size nor
peak similarity separates these phantoms from correct
detections, since a correct spleen appeared at the
0th size percentile while a wrong bladder sat at the 65th, so no per-organ gate
can remove them.

Assignment by competition can remove them, because it makes the organs contest
the same voxels rather than claim them independently. Each voxel goes to at most
one organ, the one that responds most strongly there after calibration. An
organ whose own best voxel is claimed by a stronger organ is reported
\textbf{absent} and receives no mask, which is precisely the signal independent
thresholding cannot produce. Organs stream one at a time against a running
best-value and best-label array, so memory stays bounded regardless of organ
count.

\paragraph{Calibration is required for the argmax to mean anything.}
Raw similarity is not comparable across prompts, since lungs peak near 0.18 and
femurs near 0.06, so an uncalibrated cross-organ argmax would hand nearly every
voxel to the lungs. The default \emph{scale} mode divides each organ's map by
that organ's typical peak response, putting all organs on a common ``fraction of
the response this organ ever gives'' scale. An alternative \emph{z-score}
mode computes $(\text{raw} - \mu_c)/\sigma_c$ per organ, which unlike the scale
mode corrects for baseline offset as well as scale. The z-score mode is the
variant evaluated and not adopted in \S\ref{sec:results:negatives}.

\paragraph{Bilateral pairs compete as one unit.}
Letting \org{kidney-left} and \org{kidney-right} bid against each other
reproduced the same bug one level down. The two prompts differ by a single
laterality word, the text encoder separates them poorly, both peak in nearly the
same place, and the stronger one declares the weaker absent. In an early
measurement 37\% of pairs split this way. A scan contains both sides of a pair
or neither, so presence is decided for the \emph{pair}: the winner is recorded
as a group identifier, and paired organs therefore never take territory from one
another. A split at the image midline then divides the group's won territory,
following the convention the prompts state explicitly and consistently, in
which a patient-left organ ``appears on the RIGHT side of the image''.

\paragraph{Finalization.}
An organ's mask is the connected component of its group's won territory
containing that organ's own peak voxel, subject to a floor. Winning a voxel is
not sufficient: the calibrated value must also exceed 0.6, or every voxel in the
body would be assigned to something. When a member's peak falls in its pair's
half, which is routine given the laterality confusion above, the fallback is the
largest component rather than an empty mask.

\subsection{Bone-metastasis proximity index}
\label{sec:methods:index}

The index reuses the Phase~1 model frozen and unmodified, on Spine-Mets-CT-SEG
\cite{pieper2024spinemets}: 55 patients of 3D DICOM CT with per-vertebra SEG
masks, of which 46 carry usable labels. Lesion involvement per vertebral
level comes from the collection's clinical spreadsheet, \emph{not} from the SEG
file, and the tumor mask is the union of vertebra masks for levels the
spreadsheet marks involved.

OrganPrompt is inherently 2D, so each axial slice is preprocessed exactly as in
training and run through the patch-similarity engine; the resulting per-slice
maps then stack and resample to the native in-plane resolution, giving a 3D
organ volume aligned to the CT grid. Competitive assignment
(\S\ref{sec:methods:assign}) then reduces that to mutually exclusive 3D organ
masks. Lesion voxels are vertebral bone by construction, so any organ predicted
\emph{at} the lesion is a model artifact. Those voxels are carved out of every
organ mask before scoring, or a confused prediction would fake overlap with
itself.

Each (tumor, organ) pair yields
\begin{equation}
\text{overlap} = \frac{|T \cap O|}{|T|},
\qquad
\text{proximity} = \frac{1}{1 + d_{\text{mm}}},
\qquad
\text{score} = \alpha\,\text{overlap} + \beta\,\text{proximity},
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = \beta = 0.5$; the patient's index is the maximum score over
organs, so the nearest or most-involved organ drives it. $\alpha$, $\beta$ and
the max-over-organs aggregation are fixed a priori and never fitted. Here
$d_{\text{mm}}$ is the minimum physical distance between tumor and organ voxels,
obtained from a KD-tree built over organ voxel coordinates scaled by the
volume's mm spacing and queried with the lesion's coordinates.

That choice of data structure is deliberate, and rules out a Euclidean distance
transform. The EDT allocates a float64 array over the whole volume, on the order
of 600\,MB per organ across 11 organs, purely to read a single minimum out of
it, which is enough to exhaust memory on a full-resolution scan. Both point sets
are small, so the KD-tree answers the same question exactly in a few MB. The EDT
does appear, appropriately, in the HD95 and NSD surface metrics of
\S\ref{sec:methods:data}.

All of this presumes that the organ maps themselves survive the change of
dataset, so the index is preceded by a check of the organ maps on CT-ORG
\cite{rister2020ctorg}, an independent organ-labeled CT collection, to confirm
they transfer to a protocol the model never saw.

\subsection{Per-lesion validation}
\label{sec:methods:validation}

Validating that index per patient caps out at 20 usable points, namely patients
with exactly one involved vertebral level. Scoring per \emph{lesion} instead
raises this to 180 lesions across 46 patients while still requiring one
inference pass per patient, since organ masks are what cost a pass and each mask
is scored against every lesion while it is resident. Volumes are strided by 2
along $z$ with
the slice spacing scaled to match.

The quantity scored on those lesions is the rank of the nearest anatomically
expected organ within the model's distance-ordered list of organs. A lookup
table assembled from anatomical references fixes which organs are anatomically
plausible at each vertebral level. \S\ref{sec:results:lesion} describes the
correction made to that table at T11/T12, and Appendix~\ref{app:anatomy}
documents it.

\subsection{Statistics}
\label{sec:methods:stats}

Chance is not a single constant here. Because the expected-organ set size $k$
varies by vertebral level, the chance mean rank for a lesion is the expectation
of the minimum of $k$ draws without replacement from $n=11$ organs,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[\text{rank}] = \frac{n+1}{k+1},
\end{equation}
and the reported chance is the mean of this quantity over lesions. One
consequence is worth stating plainly: \emph{correcting the expected-organ table
changes chance as well as the observed statistic}, which is why the anatomy fix
in \S\ref{sec:results:lesion} moves chance from 3.01 to 2.91.

Uncertainty around that comparison comes from a bootstrap over
\textbf{patients}, not lesions \cite{efron1994bootstrap}, drawing 5{,}000
resamples of patients with replacement and carrying all of a drawn patient's
lesions with it. Lesions
within a patient share a scan, a set of organ masks, and that patient's
particular anatomy, so treating 180 lesions as 180 independent trials would
tighten the interval by roughly the square root of the mean lesions-per-patient,
about $2\times$ here, a larger effect than any of the method changes produce.

The same respect for clustering carries into the significance test, a
permutation test with 20{,}000 permutations. The null draws a fresh random
ranking for each lesion using that lesion's own $k$, so a patient with many
lesions contributes many draws exactly as it does in the observed statistic.
Point estimates cover all rankable lesions, and the clustering correction stays
in the bootstrap interval, which is the quantity worth reading alongside
$p$.

\subsection{Pre-registration and evaluation-set discipline}
\label{sec:methods:prereg}

We specified both calibration variants in \S\ref{sec:results:negatives} in full,
including the z-score coefficient $k=2$, before running them, and report them
as run. Neither was retuned after its result was seen, and a smaller $k$ was
never tried. Revisiting that choice would require fresh pre-registration rather
than a sweep over the same evaluation set.

Exposure belongs on the record too, because it bounds how much any single
comparison here should be trusted. The 180-lesion set has now absorbed several
decisions: the calibration fit, the bilateral-grouping change, the move from
per-patient to per-lesion scoring, and the two calibration variants. It is a
finite resource, and that is the stated reason for not pursuing the residual
phantom detections further (\S\ref{sec:discussion}) rather than an assertion
that nothing remains to be found.

One correction belongs on the record as well. We originally fitted the per-organ
calibration constants on the evaluation set itself, which made every validation
number derived from them circular. They are now fit instead on the 35
Spine-Mets patients \emph{outside} the evaluation set, so that the constants and
the numbers they produce no longer share data. The correction is modest but
systematic and not uniform (10 of 11 organs rose, mean $+7.0\%$), so it
genuinely reorders the
argmax instead of canceling out of a ratio: pancreas rose 21.5\% and moved from
9th to 6th, liver from 4th to 3rd, and bladder fell from 6th to 8th. Every
number in this paper uses the corrected constants.

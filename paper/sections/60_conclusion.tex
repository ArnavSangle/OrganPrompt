\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Making patch-level text--image alignment an explicit training objective turns a
globally-aligned vision--language model into a text-prompted organ localizer,
recovering most of the localization signal for morphologically distinctive
structures without any per-pixel label at inference. The same frozen model
transfers to an unseen dataset and acquisition protocol to answer a downstream
clinical question, ranking the organ a vertebral metastasis is nearest to
invading well above chance across 180 lesions.

Two findings generalize past this pipeline. Prompt elaboration is not what makes
text supervision work: the class name carries almost all of the signal, and
multi-sentence anatomical description adds nothing measurable over a single
generic sentence. And when a text-prompted localizer fails, it fails
confidently, segmenting the wrong structure cleanly rather than signalling
uncertainty, so prompt validation belongs in any deployed system as a
first-class component.

Our method remains bounded by the $14\times14$ patch grid, by a two-class
failure that we diagnose but do not repair, and by single-cohort evaluation of
the downstream index. The next experiment is specified by the diagnosis rather
than chosen freely: scoping hard negatives to the classes they were introduced
to correct, which is the one change the failed global-negative result actually
implicates.

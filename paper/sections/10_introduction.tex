\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Dense per-voxel annotation is the binding constraint on CT segmentation. A
fully supervised model such as TotalSegmentator reaches a mean Dice of 0.943
across 104 anatomical structures \cite{wasserthal2023totalsegmentator}, but that
performance rests on expert voxel-level labels for every structure of interest.
Extending such a system to a new structure, a new protocol, or a new clinical
question means commissioning new annotation.

Vision--language pretraining suggests an alternative. Models in the CLIP family
learn visual concepts from natural-language supervision alone
\cite{radford2021clip, zhai2023siglip}, and biomedical adaptations such as
PMC-CLIP \cite{lin2023pmcclip} and BiomedCLIP \cite{zhang2025biomedclip} bring
that capability into the medical domain at scale. If a text prompt could select
a region as well as it selects a label, segmentation would need no pixels at
all.

The obstacle is that these models are trained for global alignment. The
contrastive objective relates a pooled image embedding to a pooled caption
embedding, and nothing in it requires an individual patch token to be spatially
discriminative. Reading per-patch similarity off such a model recovers almost
no localization: measured here, 0.003 mIoU. The conventional workaround is the
CAM family \cite{zhou2016cam, selvaraju2017gradcam}, which extracts spatial
maps post hoc from a classifier, but a contrastively pretrained model makes no
per-organ decision for a CAM to explain.

We make the spatial property an explicit training objective instead.
We align each of the 196 ViT patch tokens with the organ's text embedding
against the downsampled ground-truth mask, so that per-patch cosine similarity
becomes directly readable as a segmentation map at inference, with no gradients,
no classifier, and no second-stage mask refiner. We claim the following
contributions.

\textbf{A patch-level alignment objective that makes text-prompted localization
work.} On 11 organ classes from TotalSegmentator, we reach 0.653 mIoU
and 0.757 Dice using no per-pixel labels at inference. The patch loss is the
single largest contributor, taking mIoU from 0.063 to 0.307 in one step, and a
subsequent calibration correction adds a further 0.059 with no retraining at all
(\S\ref{sec:results:phase1}).

\textbf{A prompt ablation that establishes what the text is actually doing, and
what it is not.} We show that corrupting the positional content of the prompts collapses
precisely the classes that depend on laterality while leaving uniquely-positioned
organs untouched, which demonstrates the text is load-bearing rather than acting
as a class index. The same experiment shows us that elaboration is not where the value lies: a bare class name recovers 96.9\% of the full multi-sentence
anatomical description's mIoU, and a one-line generic caption matches it
outright (\S\ref{sec:results:ablation}).

\textbf{A safety-relevant failure mode.} An accidental two-word error in one of
eleven prompts produced not a degraded output but a confident, clean segmentation
of the wrong organ, costing 55 IoU points on the affected class while leaving its
bilateral partner untouched. Failures of this method are directional rather than
degradative, which is the more dangerous kind, because nothing in the output
signals that anything went wrong (\S\ref{sec:results:ablation}).

\textbf{A downstream clinical application of the frozen model.} The same weights
are carried, unmodified, onto Spine-Mets-CT-SEG \cite{pieper2024spinemets}, a
dataset and acquisition protocol never seen in training, to compute a
bone-metastasis proximity index: which organ a vertebral metastasis is closest to
invading. Validating per lesion across 180 lesions in 46 patients, we find the correct
organ ranks 1.78 on average against a chance rank of 2.91, with $p < 0.0001$
under a patient-clustered permutation test (\S\ref{sec:results:lesion}).

\textbf{Negative results with diagnosed mechanisms.} Two pre-registered
calibration variants were run and neither was adopted, each for an identified
reason rather than an unexplained regression (\S\ref{sec:results:negatives}). A
per-level analysis further shows the localization failure is not diffuse: it
concentrates in two of eleven classes, one of which never ranks first at any
level where it is anatomically correct, while winning 14 times where it is not
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}).

We also report a ground-truth error found in our own evaluation table and
corrected from anatomical literature, along with a calibration leak found and
repaired. Both changed headline numbers. Reporting them is part of what the
paper claims.
